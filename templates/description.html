Welcome to the Longform Analogy Evaluation Challenge! This challenge focuses on evaluating the quality of analogies and their associated video animations designed to explain Computer Science concepts.

Evaluation Task

Participants are invited to develop automated evaluation systems that assess how well each analogy and animation communicates a given concept. The analogies are crafted to creatively explain technical topics, and your job is to judge their accuracy and effectiveness.

Dataset

You will receive a dataset with N examples of analogies. Each example consists of:

  • A textual analogy explaining a CS concept
  • An optional animation (video) illustrating that analogy
  • A concept description used to ground evaluation
  • Reference metric scores (for dev phase only)

Challenge Format

The challenge is split into two phases:

  1. Text-only Phase: You evaluate analogies based on their textual descriptions.
  2. Text + Video Phase: You evaluate both the text and the accompanying animation.

Each submission must return metric scores for the analogies as described in the evaluation criteria.

Evaluation Criteria

Each analogy will be scored on the following six metrics:

  • Target Concept Coverage
  • Mapping Strength
  • Metaphoricity
  • Alignment with Textual Analogy (video phase only)
  • Visual Clarity/Design (video phase only)
  • Visual Engagement (video phase only)

Each metric is rated on a scale. The total score is the sum (or weighted sum) of all individual metric scores.

Submissions

You will submit your evaluation predictions in the specified JSON format. The format and further submission guidelines can be found under the "Submission Guidelines" tab.

Goal

The main goal is to explore automated techniques for evaluating long-form pedagogical analogies, especially those used in CS education. High-performing systems may combine NLP, vision, and multimodal understanding.

Why This Matters

Analogies are powerful teaching tools. Evaluating them is complex even for humans, and solving this computationally opens up possibilities for better AI-based analogy processing tools.
